Bibit.
Backoffice UI.

An internal SaaS that lets Bibit's ops team move faster than the markets they serve.

Bibit is a mutual fund app that has helped over 5 million Indonesians start investing — many of them for the very first time. The customer-facing app is the part most people see; behind it sits a sprawling backoffice that handles registrations, S-Invest reconciliation, KYC reviews, partnership flows, and incident triage.

I joined as the Lead Frontend Engineer in late 2020 to take ownership of that backoffice — shipping new feature surfaces, untangling the legacy admin, and growing a small frontend team into three squads that ship together every week.

02Problem

"Ops is faster than the tool. Every approval, every reject, every reconciliation needed three tabs and a Slack thread."

The original backoffice grew organically — six different apps stitched together by URL params, three auth providers, no shared design system, and zero front-end performance budget. New features took 6–8 weeks even for trivial CRUD work, and the ops team had built shadow tooling in Notion and spreadsheets to fill the gaps.

  • Rejected-by-S-Invest queue had no clear SLA surface
  • Partnership register required ops to copy IDs across 4 screens
  • No audit trail in-app — incidents traced through DB logs
  • Frontend bundle > 4.5 MB; LCP hovered around 6.2s

03Solution

I led a phased re-platform onto a single Next.js shell with a shared design system (Lentera), introduced a GraphQL gateway, and rebuilt the three highest-traffic flows from scratch. Releases moved from monthly to biweekly within six months, and metrics improved across the board.

04Architecture

FE-heavy by design: a monorepo of routed micro-frontends behind a single shell, talking to a GraphQL gateway. Server components handle data-bound surfaces; client components handle interactive flows.

  • A11y baked into base layer
